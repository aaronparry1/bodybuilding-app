When to actually deload.

Most programmes put a deload on the calendar every four or eight weeks, whether you need one or not. Here's why that's often wrong, and what to actually watch for instead.

Why "every 4 weeks" is a guess, not a rule

A fixed-schedule deload assumes everyone accumulates fatigue at the same rate. They don't. Someone sleeping eight hours a night, eating enough, and training three days a week can often push six or eight weeks before they need one. Someone under-recovering, stressed, or training six days a week might need one after three. A calendar doesn't know either of those things; your actual performance does.

The real signs you need one

Watch for these across two or three consecutive sessions, not one bad day:

  • Reps at your usual weight are dropping, not holding or climbing
  • The same weight feels noticeably harder than it did two weeks ago
  • Warm-up sets feel heavy, not just the top sets
  • Sleep, motivation, or joint aches are worse than usual, on top of the lifting itself feeling harder

One of these on its own is probably a bad night's sleep or a stressful week. Two or three together, holding for several sessions, is fatigue that's outrunning your recovery.

What a deload actually is

A deload isn't a rest week. Full time off loses you more than it gives back, and you lose some of the adaptation you just built. A proper deload keeps you moving with something like 40 to 60% less total volume, either fewer sets, lighter weight, or both, for about a week. You should leave every set feeling like you barely touched it.

What happens after

Come back at the same weights you were using before the deload, not lighter and not heavier. If the reps that were stalling suddenly move again, the deload worked, and you resume normal progression from there. If they're still stuck at the same place, the problem wasn't fatigue, it was something else, usually the exercise itself or your recovery outside the gym, sleep and food specifically.

The trap with fixed-schedule deloads

Taking one on a strict calendar when you don't actually need it wastes a week you could have kept progressing in. Skipping one when you do need it just delays the stall by a week and makes the eventual dip in performance worse. Neither is as good as deloading from what you're actually seeing in your training.
