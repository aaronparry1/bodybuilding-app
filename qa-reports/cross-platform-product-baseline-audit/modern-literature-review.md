# Modern scientific literature review

This is a baseline claim map, not a clinical guideline or proof that the app is scientifically validated.

| Topic/claim | Population/effect | Confidence/limitations | App use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy volume | more weekly sets generally increases growth to a point; individual response and study range limit a universal ceiling | moderate | soft starting range + response monitoring, never fixed MRV truth |
| Frequency | when volume is equated, frequency has little meaningful hypertrophy effect | moderate; scheduling/adherence matters | distribute volume for recovery/time/preference, not hard hypertrophy rule ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)) |
| Proximity to failure | momentary failure is not clearly superior; closer proximity may matter more for hypertrophy than strength | moderate, RIR often estimated | bounded heuristic; avoid mandatory failure ([2022 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/), [2024 meta-regression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/)) |
| Rep/load range | hypertrophy can occur across broad loads when effort/volume are adequate; heavier loads favor 1RM specificity | moderate | goal/exercise-specific ranges, not one “hypertrophy zone” |
| Rest | >60 s may offer small hypertrophy benefit; little detected beyond ~90 s in current synthesis | low-moderate | performance-preserving defaults with time tradeoff ([2024 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/)) |
| Exercise order | earlier exercises generally receive performance priority | moderate | preference/goal ordering heuristic, not superiority claim |
| Supersets | time-efficient; acute fatigue/performance depends on pairing | moderate, chronic evidence smaller | phase/exercise-specific option ([2025 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39903375/)) |
| Drop sets | similar hypertrophy to traditional sets, often less time | low-moderate; six studies/young adults | time-efficiency option, not superior method ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37523092/)) |
| Clusters | preserve velocity/power and reduce acute fatigue relative to traditional structures | moderate acute, limited chronic | power/quality heuristic ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32901442/)) |
| Advanced methods | no consistent hypertrophy superiority over traditional training | low-moderate, trained samples small | variety/time/quality only ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38654909/)) |
| Velocity loss | higher loss increases acute fatigue; tradeoffs differ for hypertrophy vs power | moderate, exercise/load dependent | objective stop/quality signal where measured ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36178597/)) |
| Periodisation | helpful for organizing specificity/fatigue; linear vs undulating hypertrophy differences unclear | moderate | architecture/soft policy, not brand-name dogma ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28848690/)) |
| Autoregulation | RPE/APRE/VBT can support strength; no basis for unconstrained daily rewriting | emerging | bounded load adjustment with deterministic limits ([2025 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40791980/)) |
| VBT superiority | current comparisons show imprecise/trivial differences vs traditional methods | low | optional objective signal, no marketing superiority ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35380511/)) |
| Deload/taper | taper evidence is sport/strength specific; deload practice lacks robust universal dose rules | low-moderate | conservative policy + explicit uncertainty |
| RPE/RIR reliability | useful but individual/exercise-dependent and less reliable farther from failure | moderate | optional/calibrated input, triangulate with performance |
| e1RM | useful trend estimate; equation/exercise/fatigue introduce error | moderate | confidence-tagged derived fact, never exact max |
| Readiness questionnaires | practical but noisy; associations do not prove the correct prescription | low-moderate | advisory input with decay/contradiction handling |
| Variation/weak points | specificity matters; variation can manage exposure/adherence but “weak point” inference is uncertain | low-moderate | explicit preference + evidence thresholds |
| Concurrent training | interference depends on modality, dose, proximity and goal | moderate | scheduling/recovery heuristic |
| Power | ballistic intent, specificity and fatigue management matter | moderate | lower velocity loss/quality emphasis |
| Sex/age/training age | relative principles overlap, but baseline capacity, recovery and evidence representation differ | moderate but underrepresented groups | stratify confidence; do not assume young male trained samples generalize |
| Pain/injury | training evidence cannot diagnose or prescribe rehabilitation | high boundary | stop/seek-qualified-help safety rule only |
| Adherence/missed/extra sessions | sustainable completion often dominates theoretical optimization; exact rescheduling rules under-studied | moderate principle, low rule specificity | preserve plan identity, bounded reflow, ask preferences |

Major gaps requiring formal searches/extraction: dose-response at high trained volumes; deload RCTs; taper by strength goal; objective readiness interventions; validated RIR by exercise/training age; women/older adults; missed-session algorithms; pain-boundary consensus; rest-pause chronic outcomes; extra-session risk. Every future claim needs population, intervention/comparison, effect magnitude, bias/limitations, conflicts and review date.
